Why benchmark?

Annual energy benchmarking is required for buildings covered by San Francisco EBO or California AB 802. Which program applies depends on your building’s location, size and use.

Benchmarking records your building’s energy use in the EPA’s ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager, giving you a clearer picture of how it performs. You can track changes over time, compare eligible properties with similar buildings and identify opportunities to reduce energy use.

Know what you’re filing.

Is benchmarking the same as ENERGY STAR certification?

No. Benchmarking records and reports energy performance. Eligible building types may receive an ENERGY STAR score from 1 to 100, but not every building receives one. Certification is voluntary and requires a separate application and professional verification. Filing a benchmark does not certify a building.

Which buildings report under California AB 802?

Outside CEC-exempt local benchmarking programs, statewide reporting generally applies to buildings with more than 50,000 square feet and either no residential utility accounts or 17 or more residential units, including mixed-use buildings. Reports are due June 1 for the previous calendar year. Condominiums are exempt from state reporting, and other property-specific exemptions may apply.

Does this apply to condominiums and mixed-use buildings?

The two programs differ. Condominiums are exempt from statewide AB 802 reporting, but San Francisco’s residential requirement generally starts at 50,000 square feet and can cover condominiums and mixed-use properties. A statewide exemption does not automatically exempt a building from San Francisco’s rules. We review the property’s details and reporting status.

Does a San Francisco building need a separate state filing?

A benchmark accepted by San Francisco also fulfills the applicable California AB 802 reporting requirement. A separate state filing is not needed. Submitting only to the California Energy Commission does not fulfill San Francisco’s requirement. We identify the correct reporting destination before preparing your submission.

Is an energy audit included in annual benchmarking?

Energy audits are separate from annual benchmarking. Under San Francisco’s ordinance, covered nonresidential buildings generally have a five-year audit obligation, with their own schedule and exemptions. Annual benchmarking does not replace an audit, and this service describes benchmarking preparation and reporting.

What if we have an existing Portfolio Manager account or missing data?

We can work with an existing record and review what is already in place. We’ll identify missing building details or utility data and coordinate next steps with you. Cohi helps resolve missing data and identify the reporting options available under the applicable program.
